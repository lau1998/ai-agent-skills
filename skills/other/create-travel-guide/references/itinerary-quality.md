# Itinerary Construction And Quality

## Build Each Day Completely

Give every day this structure:

- Exact date, sleep location, and daily theme.
- Ordered start and end times for each activity.
- Realistic visit or activity duration.
- Point-to-point transport mode and duration.
- Transfer, queue, weather, navigation, and delay buffers.
- Meals, hydration, and rest periods.
- Reservation, ticket, check-in, clothing, and equipment requirements.
- Safety notes tied to the traveler and date.
- A verified fallback for closure, weather, fatigue, or reduced mobility.

Do not fill a day merely because time appears open. Keep unstructured recovery time when the traveler's pace requires it.

## Handle Transition Days

- On arrival days, account for landing or station time, immigration when applicable, baggage claim, transfer, waiting, delays, lodging check-in, meals, and fatigue.
- On departure days, work backward from carrier or operator requirements and include checkout, baggage, transfer, traffic or service buffers, and terminal processing.
- On cross-city days, include checkout, baggage movement or storage, station or airport waiting, the full transport chain, delays, local transfer, and check-in.
- On hotel-change days, state where baggage stays during the gap and avoid schedules that assume rooms are available outside verified check-in or checkout times.

## Run The Global Checklist

- Remove unnecessary backtracking and cluster nearby activities without forcing incompatible hours.
- Confirm opening hours, timed entries, reservations, transit schedules, and connection buffers for exact dates.
- Keep activity volume within the confirmed pace, walking limit, mobility needs, weather exposure, and rest requirements.
- Adjust for children, seniors, pregnancy, disability, medical needs, and different energy levels within the group.
- For self-drive, check vehicle type, driver count, license suitability, daily driving and rest limits, fatigue, daylight, night-driving limits, mountain-road tolerance, weather, road conditions, closures, parking, tolls, fuel, charging, roadside coverage, and official safety advice. Treat these as constraints, not evidence that driving is safe.
- Confirm each lodging location supports the arrival, departure, current-day, and next-day routes.
- Keep core costs within budget and preserve an explicit emergency buffer.
- Include every must-do item and exclude every avoid item unless the traveler approves a change.
- Replace high-risk activities when weather, health, age, skill, equipment, or official restrictions make them unsuitable.

When route, time, budget, fatigue, mobility, or safety constraints conflict, change the actual route, schedule, activity, transport, or lodging. For self-drive, replan the route, lodging, or transport whenever confirmed limits, daylight, fatigue guidance, weather, road conditions, or official safety advice would be violated. Merely knowing the inputs never makes late or night driving safe. Never retain an impossible plan and merely annotate the conflict.

## Build A Transparent Budget

Use the traveler's confirmed total or per-person basis and currency. Separate:

- Long-distance transport.
- Local transport, rental vehicle, tolls, parking, fuel, or charging.
- Accommodation.
- Food and drink.
- Tickets and activities.
- Insurance, visa, permits, connectivity, and other preparation.
- Emergency reserve.

Label each activity `free`, `core paid`, or `optional paid`. Show quantity, unit price or verified estimate, and subtotal. Keep optional spending separate from the core trip total. When the budget is tight, remove optional items or propose verified alternatives; never force the total down with an unverified low price.
